If you are buying a home in the United States, understanding how your monthly mortgage payment is calculated can save you thousands of dollars. Most buyers only look at the interest rate, but your actual monthly payment has four components — principal, interest, property tax, and home insurance — commonly called PITI.

## The Basic Formula

Your core mortgage payment (principal and interest) is calculated using this formula:

M = P[r(1+r)^n]/[(1+r)^n-1]

Where P is your loan amount, r is your monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12), and n is your total number of payments (loan term in years multiplied by 12).

## Real Example

Home price: $400,000. Down payment: 20% ($80,000). Loan amount: $320,000. Interest rate: 6.5%. Loan term: 30 years. Monthly principal and interest: $2,022. Add average property tax ($400/month), home insurance ($100/month), and HOA fees ($150/month) and your total monthly payment becomes $2,672.

## Why the Down Payment Matters

A 20% down payment eliminates Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI), which typically costs 0.5% to 1.5% of the loan amount annually. On a $320,000 loan that is up to $400 per month in extra costs. Putting down less than 20% significantly increases your monthly payment.

## Fixed vs Adjustable Rate

A fixed rate mortgage keeps your principal and interest payment the same for the entire loan term. An adjustable rate mortgage (ARM) starts lower but can increase after an initial fixed period, typically 5 or 7 years. For most buyers in 2026, a fixed rate provides more financial certainty.
