Choosing between a fixed and variable rate mortgage is one of the most important financial decisions you will make when buying a home. The wrong choice can cost thousands of dollars over the life of your loan.

## What is a Fixed Rate Mortgage

A fixed rate mortgage locks in your interest rate for the entire loan term, typically 15 or 30 years. Your principal and interest payment never changes, regardless of what happens to interest rates in the economy.

This predictability makes budgeting simple and protects you if rates rise significantly after you buy.

## What is a Variable Rate Mortgage

A variable rate mortgage (called an adjustable rate mortgage or ARM in the USA) has an interest rate that changes periodically based on a market index. A common structure is a 5/1 ARM — fixed for the first 5 years, then adjusting annually.

Variable rates typically start lower than fixed rates, which means lower initial payments.

## When Fixed Rate Makes Sense

Choose fixed when rates are historically low and likely to rise. Choose fixed when you plan to stay in the home long term. Choose fixed when you need payment certainty for budgeting. In the current high-rate environment, many buyers lock in now expecting to refinance if rates fall.

## When Variable Rate Makes Sense

Choose variable when you plan to sell or refinance within 5 to 7 years before the rate adjusts. Choose variable when rates are high and expected to fall. Choose variable when the initial payment savings are significant and you can absorb future increases.

## Real Cost Comparison

$320,000 loan. Fixed rate 6.5% over 30 years: monthly payment $2,022, total interest $408,000. 5/1 ARM starting at 5.5%, adjusting to 7.5% after year 5: initial payment $1,817, but payment jumps to $2,270 after adjustment, total interest potentially exceeds fixed rate cost.
