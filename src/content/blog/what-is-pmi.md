Private Mortgage Insurance, commonly called PMI, is one of the most misunderstood costs of buying a home in the USA. Many buyers are caught off guard when they discover it adds hundreds of dollars to their monthly payment.

## What is PMI

PMI is insurance that protects the lender, not you, if you default on your loan. Lenders require it when your down payment is less than 20% of the home's purchase price because a smaller down payment represents higher risk for the lender.

## How Much Does PMI Cost

PMI typically costs between 0.5% and 1.5% of your loan amount per year. On a $320,000 loan, that is $1,600 to $4,800 per year, or $133 to $400 per month added to your mortgage payment. The exact rate depends on your credit score, loan amount, and down payment size.

## When Can You Remove PMI

Under the Homeowners Protection Act, your lender must automatically cancel PMI when your loan balance reaches 78% of the original home value. You can also request cancellation when you reach 80% loan-to-value through payments or home appreciation. To request early cancellation, contact your lender and you may need a home appraisal.

## How to Avoid PMI Completely

The simplest way is to make a 20% down payment. On a $400,000 home that means saving $80,000 before buying. Alternatively, some lenders offer piggyback loans (an 80/10/10 structure) where you take a second loan to cover part of the down payment and avoid PMI. VA loans for eligible veterans require no down payment and no PMI.

## Is PMI Always Bad

Not necessarily. If home prices are rising rapidly, buying sooner with a smaller down payment and paying PMI temporarily might build more equity than waiting to save 20%. The decision depends on your local market and financial situation.
