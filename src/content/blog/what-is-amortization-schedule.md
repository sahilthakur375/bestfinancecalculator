An amortization schedule is a complete table showing every payment you will make over the life of your loan, broken down into how much goes toward interest and how much reduces your principal balance. Understanding it can change how you think about your mortgage.

## Why Your Early Payments Are Mostly Interest

This surprises most homeowners. In the first year of a 30-year mortgage, the majority of every payment goes to interest, not reducing your loan balance. On a $320,000 loan at 6.5%, your first payment of $2,022 breaks down as roughly $1,733 in interest and only $289 in principal. By year 25, that same payment is $1,100 principal and $922 interest.

## How Amortization Works

Each month, interest is calculated on your remaining balance. Because your balance decreases slightly each month, the interest portion also decreases slightly, and the principal portion increases. This gradual shift is called amortization.

## Real Example Over 30 Years

Loan amount: $320,000. Rate: 6.5%. Year 1: you pay $24,264 total, $20,733 goes to interest, only $3,531 reduces your balance. Year 10: balance is down to $285,000. Year 20: balance is down to $218,000. Year 30: balance reaches zero.

## Why This Matters for Your Financial Decisions

Knowing your amortization schedule helps you decide whether to make extra payments, refinance, or sell. If you sell after 5 years without extra payments, you have barely reduced your principal. Many homeowners are surprised how little equity they have built in the early years.

## How Extra Payments Change Everything

Making one extra payment per year on a 30-year mortgage can cut your loan term by 4 to 5 years and save tens of thousands in interest. The amortization schedule shows you exactly where that money goes.
