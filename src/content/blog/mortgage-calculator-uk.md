Buying a home in the United Kingdom involves different rules and terminology compared to the USA or Australia. Understanding how your monthly mortgage payment is calculated helps you plan your finances before approaching a lender.

## How UK Mortgages Work

In the UK, mortgages are typically offered on a repayment basis (where you pay both principal and interest each month) or an interest-only basis (where you only pay interest and repay the full loan at the end). Most residential buyers use repayment mortgages.

UK lenders calculate your monthly payment using the same standard amortization formula used globally: your loan amount, interest rate, and term determine your fixed monthly payment.

## Real Example

Property price: £300,000. Deposit: 10% (£30,000). Loan amount: £270,000. Interest rate: 4.5%. Term: 25 years. Monthly repayment: approximately £1,500 per month.

Over 25 years, you repay the full £270,000 plus approximately £180,000 in interest, making the total cost of the property around £450,000.

## Stamp Duty Land Tax

In England and Northern Ireland, you pay Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) when buying a property. For first-time buyers, properties up to £425,000 are exempt. Above that threshold, rates increase progressively. Scotland uses Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) and Wales uses Land Transaction Tax (LTT) with slightly different rates.

## Fixed vs Tracker Rates in the UK

Most UK buyers choose a fixed rate deal lasting 2 or 5 years. After the fixed period ends, you are moved to the lender's standard variable rate (SVR), which is typically higher. Most homeowners remortgage at this point to get a new fixed deal.

A tracker mortgage follows the Bank of England base rate plus a set percentage. When the base rate rises, your payment rises. When it falls, your payment falls.

## How Much Can You Borrow

UK lenders typically allow you to borrow 4 to 4.5 times your annual income. A combined household income of £60,000 means you can borrow approximately £240,000 to £270,000. Some lenders go up to 5.5 times income for high earners.
