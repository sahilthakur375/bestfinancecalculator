One of the most common questions first-time buyers ask is how much house they can actually afford. Lenders use specific rules to calculate this, and understanding them before you apply saves time and prevents disappointment.

## The 28/36 Rule

Most US lenders use the 28/36 rule as a baseline. Your monthly mortgage payment should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income. Your total monthly debt payments (mortgage plus car loans, student loans, credit cards) should not exceed 36% of your gross monthly income.

Example: Gross monthly income of $7,000. Maximum mortgage payment: $1,960 (28%). Maximum total debt: $2,520 (36%).

## Debt to Income Ratio

Lenders calculate your debt-to-income ratio (DTI) by dividing your total monthly debt payments by your gross monthly income. Most lenders require a DTI below 43%. Some programs like FHA loans allow up to 50% DTI.

## How Your Down Payment Affects Affordability

A larger down payment reduces your loan amount, eliminates PMI if you reach 20%, and lowers your monthly payment. On a $400,000 home, the difference between a 10% and 20% down payment saves approximately $200 to $300 per month.

## Credit Score Impact

Your credit score directly affects your interest rate. A score of 760 or above gets you the best rates. A score of 620 might still qualify for a loan but at a rate 1 to 2 percentage points higher, costing tens of thousands more over the loan term.

## A Realistic Affordability Example

Annual income: $80,000. Monthly gross income: $6,667. Maximum mortgage payment (28%): $1,867. With a 6.5% rate and 30-year term, that payment supports a loan of approximately $295,000. With a 20% down payment, that means a home price of around $370,000.
