Personal loans and mortgages are both ways to borrow money, but they work very differently. Understanding which is right for your situation can save you significant money in interest.

## What is a Mortgage

A mortgage is a secured loan specifically for purchasing or refinancing real estate. The property itself serves as collateral, meaning the lender can repossess your home if you stop making payments. Because the loan is secured, lenders offer much lower interest rates than unsecured loans.

Mortgage terms typically range from 15 to 30 years, and loan amounts can reach into the hundreds of thousands or millions of dollars.

## What is a Personal Loan

A personal loan is an unsecured loan, meaning no collateral is required. Lenders approve you based on your credit score and income alone. Because there is no collateral, interest rates are significantly higher than mortgage rates.

Personal loan terms typically range from 1 to 7 years, and amounts are usually limited to $50,000 or less.

## Key Differences

Interest rates: mortgages typically 4% to 7%, personal loans typically 8% to 25%. Loan terms: mortgages 15 to 30 years, personal loans 1 to 7 years. Loan amounts: mortgages hundreds of thousands, personal loans typically under $50,000. Collateral: mortgages require property as security, personal loans require none. Approval time: mortgages take weeks, personal loans can fund within days.

## When to Use a Personal Loan Instead of a Mortgage

Personal loans make sense for home renovations when the amount is small (under $30,000) and you do not want to refinance your mortgage. They also work for debt consolidation, emergency expenses, or purchases where speed of funding matters more than the lowest possible rate.
