Buying your first home in Australia is one of the biggest financial decisions of your life. This guide covers everything you need to know before signing anything.

## How Much Deposit Do You Need

Most Australian lenders require a minimum 5% deposit, but 20% is strongly recommended to avoid Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI). On a $700,000 property in Sydney, a 5% deposit is $35,000 but you will pay LMI of approximately $15,000 to $20,000 on top. A 20% deposit ($140,000) eliminates LMI entirely.

## First Home Owner Grant (FHOG)

The First Home Owner Grant is a one-off payment from the state government to eligible first home buyers. The amount varies by state — typically $10,000 in NSW and Victoria, up to $30,000 in Queensland for new builds in regional areas. The grant applies to new homes or substantially renovated homes, not established properties in most states.

## Stamp Duty Concessions for First Home Buyers

Each state offers stamp duty concessions or exemptions for first home buyers. In NSW, first home buyers pay no stamp duty on properties up to $800,000 and a reduced rate up to $1,000,000. In Victoria, first home buyers are exempt on properties up to $600,000. Always check your state revenue office for current thresholds as they change regularly.

## The First Home Guarantee Scheme

The federal government's First Home Guarantee allows eligible buyers to purchase with just a 5% deposit without paying LMI. The government guarantees the remaining 15% to the lender. There are limited places available each financial year so apply early.

## How Much Can You Borrow

Australian lenders assess your borrowing capacity based on your income, existing debts, living expenses, and number of dependants. As a rough guide, most borrowers can access approximately 5 to 6 times their annual gross income, though this varies significantly between lenders and individual circumstances.

## Your Monthly Repayments

On a $560,000 loan (80% of $700,000) at 6.0% over 30 years, your monthly repayment is approximately $3,357. Switching to fortnightly repayments of $1,549 pays the loan off 4 years earlier and saves approximately $60,000 in interest.
